## Platform

web

## Users

Primary users: tarot readers, esoteric bloggers/authors, Tarot schools,
authors of original methodologies, and artists/creators who want to release
a commercial Tarot or Oracle deck under their own name.

Most valuable segment: creators who already monetize an audience through
consultations, courses, or digital products and want to add a physical
product.

Explicitly out of scope: someone who just wants 20–50 decks printed for
personal use. The site should not read as a print shop serving that need.

## Product Purpose

LunaLum Deck Studio designs, produces, and sells original Tarot/Oracle
decks. It does two things from one capability: (1) sells its own commercial
decks, and (2) creates bespoke, commercial-grade decks for clients,
end-to-end — idea → concept → visual style → illustrations → card design →
packaging → production prep → sample → print run, with optional guidebook,
inserts, QR codes, and a digital component.

Success for a client engagement = going from an idea (or no idea yet) to a
finished, physically printed, commercial-quality deck in hand — not a
design file, not a print quote.

## Positioning

LunaLum is not a printing house ("choose size, paper, print run") and not a
design studio ("send us your idea, we'll draw the cards"). Their proof is
literally in front of the client: LunaLum makes and sells its own
commercial decks, and now offers that same proven path to other creators.

Core claim (client-facing, treat as durable): "We've already gone through
the whole path with our own decks. Now we can go through it with yours."
This lets them sell a finished product outcome, not printing capacity or
design hours.

Three real alternatives clients weigh instead of LunaLum:

1. **Printers/manufacturers** (including factories in China) — print well,
   but the client must already arrive with a finished, production-ready
   product.
2. **Illustrators/design studios** — create the visual part, but
   production and commercial launch remain the author's own problem.
3. **DIY** — find an artist, a designer, a factory; handle materials,
   proofs, packaging, and logistics themselves.

LunaLum's claim is unifying all three into one path: "from idea to a deck
you can hold in your hands."

## Operating Context

Client-facing production pipeline: idea → concept → visual style →
illustrations → card design → packaging → production prep → sample → print
run. Optional add-ons: guidebook, inserts, QR/digital mechanics.

Intended visitor flow (durable, not a page layout decision): show the real,
finished LunaLum decks and production detail *first* — cards, print
detail, packaging, existing visual worlds — and only after that, offer the
service ("now let's make yours"). The site sells the result before it sells
the service.

Primary conversion action: a single primary CTA — "Хочу свою колоду" /
"Обсудить свою колоду" ("I want my own deck" / "Let's discuss my deck") —
opening a short intake form, not a full brief:

- Name
- Link to their blog/channel
- Audience size
- Whether they already have a deck idea
- Short description of the idea, if any

Explicitly stated: do not require a full spec/brief at this stage — the
goal is to start a conversation, not qualify a finished RFP.

**Open item:** the listed form fields don't include an email/phone/contact
method — confirm whether one is missing from the brief or intentionally
deferred to the conversation itself before building the form.

## Capabilities and Constraints

Confirmed capabilities: Tarot decks, Oracle decks, original/custom deck
structures, illustration, guidebooks, box/packaging design, foil stamping,
gilded edges, unusual materials, inserts, QR/digital mechanics.

Pricing is deliberately not published. It depends on concept complexity,
print run size, and the launch's financial model. The only pricing
statement the site should make: "Стоимость рассчитывается индивидуально и
зависит от концепции и тиража" ("Price is calculated individually,
depending on concept and print run"). No number, and no "from ₽X" framing
— that reads as a budget print shop, which is explicitly what to avoid.

Perceived price tier: premium. The site must not create a "cheap service"
impression at any point.

## Brand Commitments

**Name:** LunaLum Deck Studio.

**Brand character** (explicit, binding): modern, beautiful, a bit
mystical, bold, visually rich, expensive-feeling. Explicitly **not** a
stereotypical "witch shop" — minimize obvious esoteric clichés (endless
moons, candles, purple glow, pseudo-gothic). Target reference category:
premium fashion / collectible-object / art-studio-that-specializes-in-Tarot
— not a generic "esoteric brand." Stated reasoning: the magic already lives
inside the decks themselves; the site doesn't need to additionally "shout"
esotericism.

**Tone of voice** (explicit, binding): short, confident, human. Example of
what the voice should *not* sound like: «Наша команда профессионалов
поможет реализовать вашу уникальную концепцию на всех этапах создания
продукта».

Verbatim voice/copy examples given as binding reference (real candidate
copy, not just descriptions of tone):

- «Создадим твою колоду. От первой идеи до готового тиража.»
- «Есть идея? Покажи. Нет идеи? Придумаем вместе.»
- «От первой карты до готовой коробки.»
- «Мы сделали свои. Теперь сделаем твою.»
- «Следующая может быть твоей.»
- CTA copy: «Хочу свою колоду» / «Обсудить свою колоду» / «ХОЧУ СВОЮ
  КОЛОДУ»

Stated principle: "Текст вторичен. Продукт должен продавать глазами."
(Copy is secondary — the product must sell through imagery.)

**Open item:** all voice examples were given in Russian, but the site's
language was never explicitly stated. Confirm whether the site is
Russian-only, Russian-first with an English version, or something else
before copywriting begins — this is inferred, not confirmed.

## Product Principles

1. Show the result before the service: real decks and production detail
   come first; the offer to create a custom deck comes second.
2. Sell a finished commercial physical product outcome — not printing
   capacity, not design hours.
3. Avoid every category cliché on sight: no printing-house layout (service
   grid / steps / testimonials / contact form), no marketplace-style
   product grid, no stereotypical esoteric/witchy visual language.
4. The studio's own decks are the primary proof. No invented testimonials,
   client logos, or case studies beyond what's real.
5. Premium perception is non-negotiable: no visible pricing, no "from ₽"
   framing, nothing that reads as a discount service.
