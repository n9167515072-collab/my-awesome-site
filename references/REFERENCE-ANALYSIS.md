# Reference analysis (final, screenshot-corrected)

This supersedes the scattered description-only analysis from the first
pass. Per-reference facts below are marked **confirmed** (verified against
a real screenshot in `references/screenshots/`) or **unconfirmed**
(description/research only — no screenshot on hand). Where a screenshot
corrected an earlier description-only assumption, that's called out
explicitly, because it changed real conclusions (see
`references/TASTE-PROFILE.md`).

## SSSolitaire (sssolitaire.com, Six Socks Studio) — confirmed

Warm, light putty/cream background — **not** dark, contrary to the
original description-only guess. A single terracotta/orange collaged
playing card (King of Hearts) sits at a raked angle with a soft shadow and
a rotating "click and drag" badge. A giant, ghosted, low-contrast serif
wordmark ("SSSOLITAIRE") fills the background as pure texture, not as
readable copy. Small, elegant serif headlines ("OUR CARDS" / "YOUR MOVE")
bookend the composition top-left and bottom-right at matching scale.

Takeaway: typography can act as a huge-scale background texture that
doesn't compete with the object, distinct from typography-as-hero
(Locomotive, below).

## The Cool Club x FWA (Wonderland) — confirmed

Dozens of cards tiled edge-to-edge in an isometric field, each with its
own distinct illustrated world (different artist/style/color per card).
One card (King of Hearts, a samurai/sword illustration on black) is
enlarged and focused center-frame with "Video"/"Discuss" actions beneath
it and chevron navigation either side. Chrome is minimal — a thin header/
footer only.

Takeaway: wildly different visual worlds per-card stay legible as one
system purely through consistent spatial geometry (angle, scale, shadow)
— not through a shared palette. Selecting one object among many works as
navigation into a focused state.

## Theory11 (theory11.com) — product photography confirmed, site UI unconfirmed

The two images on hand are product/box photography, not the site's actual
page layout (never seen). Confirmed: black background, gold/bronze foil
with architectural/engraved linework, warm directional raking light that
catches the foil specifically, shallow depth of field. Visual vocabulary
reads as "engraved book binding," not "occult ornament."

Takeaway: the specific recipe for "expensive" here is concrete —
engraved linework + directional warm light + shallow focus on 2–3cm of
material — not an abstraction.

## Lusion (lusion.co) — confirmed, corrects earlier assumption

The page itself is **light** (pale lavender background) — not dark, as
originally guessed. The dark tone lives only inside a contained,
rounded-corner "stage": a card/frame holding a black scene where glossy
blue/white/black 3D forms float. Typography is a restrained neutral
grotesk; nav is pill-shaped with small dot icons. Two captured frames look
like the same hero moment (object mid-animation), not confirmed to be
genuinely different scroll states.

Takeaway: dark/light contrast here is created **locally**, via a
contained object-stage, not by theming the whole page dark.

## Locomotive (locomotive.ca) — confirmed (footer section only)

The captured screen is the contact/footer ("We should talk"), not the
homepage hero. Near-black (warm charcoal, not pure black), a huge thin
sans-serif headline stacked across three lines, hairline rules as the
only structural device (no cards/boxes), a small logo mark, and a
floating circular CTA. Confirms the original description-only read:
typography as the sole hero, discipline over decoration.

## Koto (koto.studio) — unconfirmed, no screenshot

No image was provided. Nothing here should be cited as visual-taste
evidence. Kept only as a **business-architecture** reference: portfolio →
impression of skill level → understanding it's a service → wanting one's
own → inquiry, with the conversion feeling embedded in the experience
rather than a hard switch into a B2B pitch.

## Caeli Energie (caeli-energie.com, agency Beaucoup) — confirmed, corrects earlier assumption

The captured hero is **not** an empty/void "museum" staging as originally
guessed — it's a full-bleed **real photograph** of a forest/sky
environment, with a light cream panel floating in front containing bold,
tightly-tracked deep-green display type ("LA RÉVOLUTION FRAÎCHE") and a
small centered product render beneath it with a "Découvrir" pill.

Takeaway: this mixes real environmental photography with a clean
typographic panel — not a content-free void around a sculptural object.
The "object in a controlled, quiet composition" read still holds; "empty
void" does not.

## Anti-reference: generic tarot/esoteric site (no single URL — a category)

Confirmed by description only (not a real site under analysis, but a
pattern to avoid): black/navy background, stars, moon, zodiac wheel, gold
serif, glowing cards, purple gradients, mystical particles, "enter the
mystical world" copy. Cross-checked against the confirmed references
above: a dark background is not itself the problem (Lusion's object-stage,
Theory11's product photography are both dark and neither reads as
esoteric) — the problem is specifically the combination of dark +
stars/moon/zodiac/glow vocabulary.
