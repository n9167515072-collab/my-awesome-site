LunaLum — Visual Reference Pack

These references are inspiration, not designs to copy. The purpose is to extract principles of composition, object treatment, interaction, typography, motion, and portfolio-to-service conversion.

01 — SSSolitaire

Suggested filename: 01-sssolitaire-hero.jpg

Use for: physical cards becoming part of the interface; card as tactile object; editorial scale.

What matters:

the card behaves like an object, not a product thumbnail

the interface feels like a surface / scene rather than a stack of sections

digital interaction continues the physical nature of the product

Do not copy: beige palette, exact type treatment, solitaire/game UI.

02–03 — FWA × The Cool Club

Suggested filenames:

02-coolclub-field.jpg

03-coolclub-selected-card.jpg

Use for: many cards existing in one spatial field; selecting one object from many; depth and object-led navigation.

What matters:

many objects can coexist without becoming a conventional grid

selecting a card becomes navigation

each card can represent its own visual world

Do not copy: exact 54-card fan/spread, exact selection mechanic, FWA visual language.

04–05 — Theory11 / premium card craft

Suggested filenames:

04-theory11-release.jpg

05-theory11-macro.jpg

Use for: perceived value of a physical deck; macro details; foil, embossing, paper, edges, packaging.

What matters:

a deck is presented as a collectible release, not an SKU

materials sell quality before copy does

macro photography can become a narrative moment

Do not copy: occult/secret-society aesthetics, classic luxury e-commerce layout.

06–07 — Lusion

Suggested filenames:

06-lusion-hero.jpg

07-lusion-scroll-state.jpg

Use for: website as one continuous scene; realtime 3D; scroll-driven state changes; technical confidence.

What matters:

motion is part of the story, not entrance decoration

one spatial world can continue through several states

interaction can make the central object feel alive

Do not copy: sci-fi studio aesthetic, specific 3D shapes, WebGL for its own sake.

08–09 — Locomotive

Suggested filenames:

08-locomotive-type.jpg

09-locomotive-work-list.jpg

Use for: typography as composition; extreme scale contrast; editorial confidence; minimal UI decoration.

What matters:

type can have physical weight in a composition

large type + disciplined structure can feel more expensive than decoration

portfolio navigation does not need cards/tiles

Do not copy: exact typefaces, exact agency listing structure.

10 — Koto

Suggested filename: 10-koto-portfolio.jpg

Use for: business architecture rather than style.

What matters:

work is the main sales argument

the transition from “I am looking at great work” to “I want them to make mine” feels natural

service conversion is embedded in the portfolio experience

Do not copy: corporate agency tone, generic case-study structure.

11–12 — Caeli Energie

Suggested filenames:

11-caeli-hero.jpg

12-caeli-object.jpg

Use for: restrained product theatre; one central sculptural object; limited palette; scroll controlling object state.

What matters:

the object gets visual priority and room to breathe

a small number of colors can carry the whole scene

technical product can be treated almost like a museum object

Do not copy: green/cream palette, one-product site structure.

Anti-reference

Avoid the conventional tarot/esoteric website language:

stars / moon / zodiac wheel

black + gold as automatic “premium tarot” shorthand

purple mystical gradients

glowing cards / particles

ornate mystical serif simply because the product is Tarot

“enter the mystical world” copy

Tarot is the product category, not the visual style.

Notes for Claude

When analysing these references, separate three things:

Taste — recurring qualities the user appears to like.

Product opportunity — techniques that may fit LunaLum specifically.

Concept hypothesis — a concrete proposed mechanic or narrative that is not yet approved.

Do not turn one reference mechanic into a rule for the site. Do not average all references into an Awwwards-style collage.
